Abstract
Pretrained time-series foundation models (TSFMs) provide
transferable representations for classification, but deployed
systems often face operating conditions that arrive sequen-
tially after training. Each new condition may provide only
a small labeled calibration set, and condition identity may
be unknown at inference. Fine-tuning shared parameters can
recovermacro-F 1onthenewconditionbutcandegradeprevi-

On a bearing-fault benchmark, a classifier built on a frozen
TSFM reaches0.997macro-F 1 on its source condition,
while its macro-F1 falls to0.43after an operating-condition
change. Fine-tuning can recover performance on the new
condition,butitchangessharedparametersandcandegrade
earlier ones. Regularization-based continual-learning meth-
ods constrain this degradation, but remain subject to the
stability–plasticitytrade-off:weakconstraintspermitadapta-
tionbutallowforgetting,whereasstrongconstraintsretainthe
source model but limit recovery on the new condition (Kirk-
patrick et al. 2017; Aljundi et al. 2018; Zenke, Poole, and
Ganguli2017;LiandHoiem2017).Expandablemethodsre-
duce parameter interference by allocating condition-specific
modules,butcommonlydependonhardrouting,explicittask
cues, or reliable inferred condition identity (Wang, Huang,
and Hong 2022). Such routing can be unreliable when con-
ditions are mixed and unlabeled at inference.
These limitations motivate a more restrictive deployment
objective: adapt to each newly observed condition without
modifyingthepretrainedencoder,theclassifierhead,orcor-
rectionslearnedforearlierconditions.Freezingthehostpre-
vents adaptation from overwriting its representation or deci-
sion geometry. It also isolates any remaining degradation to
the newly introduced correction and its routing mechanism,
rather than distributing it across shared model parameters.
Operating-condition shift is locally concentrated.We
investigate how the embedding space of a frozen TSFM
changes across operating conditions. The displacement is
highlynon-uniform:asubsetofrepresentationregionsmoves
substantially, while many regions remain comparatively sta-
ble.Asingleglobaltransformationcanthereforealterembed-
dingsthatrequirelittleornocorrection.Iftheseembeddings
already lie on the correct side of the frozen classifier’s deci-
sionboundary,unnecessarydisplacementcanreduceperfor-
mance on previously well-classified conditions. Across the
shifts we examine, regions that require little correction are
also the ones most exposed to damage from a single global
transform. This observation suggests that forgetting is not
only a consequence of learning new conditions, but also of
applyingadaptationataninappropriatelycoarsegranularity.
Piecewise adaptation with a frozen host.Motivated by
thisobservation,weintroducePACT(PiecewiseAdaptation
viaChartTransport), a frozen-host method for sequential
operating-conditionadaptation.PACTcoversthesourceem-
beddingspacewithoverlappinglocalregions,termedcharts.
For each newly observed condition, it learns a lightweight
residual transport for each chart using only a small labeled
calibration set. The transports are identity-initialized and
optimized through the frozen classifier head, so adaptation
moves shifted embeddings toward the decision geometry al-
ready represented by the host rather than modifying that ge-
ometry. Once learned, each transport is frozen; subsequent
conditions add new corrections without changing the en-
coder, head, or previous transports.
At inference, the condition identity is unknown. PACT
therefore uses a gate calibrated from in-distribution embed-
dings to softly route each sample across the accumulated
transports. An explicit identity path is always retained, pre-
serving access to the original frozen representation. This
design separates two sources of degradation. Overwriting
of the host and earlier transports is excluded because these
components never change. A prediction can differ from the
original only through the routing and transport applied to
its embedding. In particular, identity routing reproduces the
originalpredictionexactly,andwederiveamargincondition
underwhichevenanon-identityroutedcorrectionleavesthe
predicted class unchanged.
Routing is essential rather than auxiliary. Per-chart trans-
ports without a gate can improve some shifts but harm oth-
ers by applying unnecessary corrections. In several config-
urations, ungated correction performs below the unadapted
sourcemodel.PACTthereforecombineslocalcorrectionand
identity-preserving routing as a single adaptation mecha-
nism.

Our contributions are as follows:
•We formulate sequential operating-condition adaptation
for frozen TSFM classifiers under few labeled calibra-
tion samples, no condition identity at inference, and the
requirement to retain all previously observed conditions.
•Weshowthatoperating-conditionshiftislocallyconcen-
trated in the frozen embedding space and provide empir-
ical evidence that global correction can damage regions
requiring little adaptation.
•We introduce PACT, which combines identity-initialized
per-charttransportswithsoft,identity-preservingrouting
while keeping the encoder, classifier head, and previous
transports frozen. We also derive a margin-based condi-
tion under which the original prediction is preserved.
•We evaluate PACT across bearing-fault diagnosis,
human-activity recognition, and sleep-staging streams
with multiple TSFM backbones. PACT consistently im-
provesnew-conditionperformancewhileretainingearlier
conditions, and our ablations show that routing is neces-
sary for reliable piecewise adaptation.
Related Work
Domain and test-time adaptation.Cross-domain fault-
diagnosis methods align source and target representations
with adversarial losses, discrepancy matching, or multi-
source adaptation (Ganin et al. 2016; Rahman et al. 2020;
Zheng et al. 2024), while test-time adaptation updates a de-
ployed model from unlabeled samples using entropy or nor-
malization objectives (Wang et al. 2021, 2022; Song et al.
2023; Niu et al. 2022). These methods adapt shared compo-
nentstowardthecurrentdistribution.PACTinsteadassumes
test samples may come from any previously seen condition
and therefore leaves the encoder and head unchanged.
Continual and frozen-backbone learning.
Regularization-based continual learning constrains pa-
rameter drift (Kirkpatrick et al. 2017; Aljundi et al. 2018;
Zenke, Poole, and Ganguli 2017; Li and Hoiem 2017),
and expandable methods allocate routed capacity through
prompts or adapters (Wang, Huang, and Hong 2022; Yu
et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2025). Frozen-backbone methods
such as RanPAC, ADAM, and EASE construct classifiers or
subspaces on top of fixed representations (McDonnell et al.
2023; Zhou et al. 2025, 2024), mostly for class-incremental
learning. Time-series adaptation has focused on forecasting
or anomaly detection via normalization, online updates,
or unlabeled test-time objectives (Pham et al. 2023; Kim
et al. 2022; Kim, Park, and Choo 2024). PACT differs by
addressing fixed-label operating-condition streams with few
labels, no condition identity at inference, no replay, and an
explicit identity route.

Benchmarks.We evaluate on three time-series classifi-

Protocols.We use two protocols. Thepairwiseproto-

a dataset sequentially, so that forgetting on earlier domains
is measured directly after the full stream.

Ablation:TheGateIsaNecessity,NotaRefinement
The FD component ablation isolates each component.
Macro-F1 rises monotonically from the frozen head
(0.574/0.535for TSLANet/MOMENT), to full fine-tuning
(0.640), to ungated adapters (0.748/0.879), to full PACT
(0.978/0.984)—but only PACT keeps forgetting near zero;
full fine-tuning and ungated adapters recover performance
at the cost of largeF(up to0.283and0.113respectively).
Adapters supply plasticity; the gate is what converts that
plasticity into forgetting-free accumulation.
Gateablationsexplainwhy.Soft-mixtureroutingmatches
or beats hardarg maxrouting and sits just below the oracle
(perfect-routing) ceiling. By Proposition 1, the gap between
soft and oracle is thesolechannel through which PACT can
forget; that gap is 0.004/0.033/0.001 on FD/HHAR/Sleep-
EDF with TSLANet, and the larger HHAR gap is precisely
where routing is hardest—localizing the residual forgetting
to a single, interpretable cause rather than to backbone drift.
The full ablation figure and tables are in the supplementary
material.

Conclusion
We introduced PACT, a frozen-host method for sequential
operating-conditionadaptationoftime-seriesclassifiers.The
key observation is that distribution shift is locally concen-
trated in the embedding space: some regions move substan-
tiallywhileothersremainstable.PACTexploitsthisbylearn-
inglightweight,identity-initializedtransportsonlocalcharts
andsoftlyroutingtestsamplesthroughtheaccumulatedatlas,
with the source identity path always retained. The encoder,
classifier head, and all previously learned transports remain
frozen,soforgettingcanariseonlythroughgatemisrouting—
a single, bounded, and interpretable channel.
Across bearing-fault diagnosis, human-activity recogni-
tion, and sleep-staging streams with seven backbones span-
ning task-specialist encoders and frozen time-series founda-
tionmodels,PACTachievesthestrongestaverageretention–
adaptation trade-off in most settings, while retaining con-
sistently low forgetting. On the FD benchmark with frozen
MOMENT, it maintains0.984average macro-F1 after four
sequentialconditionswithjust5%calibrationlabelsperstep
andnoconditionidentityattesttime.TheHHAR/MOMENT
case,whereLwFattainsslightlyhighermacro-F 1withhigher
forgetting,marksthemainexceptionratherthanbeinghidden
by the aggregate trend.
Themethodhasclearlimits.Whenthenumberofdomains
is very large and calibration per domain is extremely sparse,
the prototype gate may not separate overlapping distribu-
